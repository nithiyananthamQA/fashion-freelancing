Inside the document

Six pages that answer every question a factory would ask.

Page through a real one — a men's polo, exactly as it went to the factory.

Page one

Technical flats

Front, back and detail line drawings of your garment, drawn to proportion. Why the factory needs it: this is the picture they cut from — ambiguity here becomes a wrong sample later.

Page two

Construction callouts

Seam class, stitch type, SPI, finish and tolerance, pinned to the exact spot on the garment. Why the factory needs it: construction is where two quotes differ most — precise callouts mean comparable prices.

Page three

Bill of materials

Every fabric and trim named — composition, GSM or count, placement, supplier and colour against each. Why the factory needs it: the BOM is what their sourcing team buys from, and one missing trim stalls the whole order.

Page four

Measurement spec

Full points of measure, graded across the size run, with a minus and plus tolerance on each. Why the factory needs it: this is the difference between “fits like the sample” and a size run nobody can wear.

Page five

Colourways & artwork

Every colourway against a Pantone TCX or fabric reference, with print, embroidery and placement artwork. Why the factory needs it: “that blue” is not a colour. A Pantone code is.

Page six

Labels & packaging

Care content and wash symbols, brand and size label placement, polybag and the carton dieline your packer folds from. Why the factory needs it: compliance lives here — and so does the unboxing your customer sees.

Straight answer

Worth it for some brands. Not for all of them.

Buy this if

  • You are sending a style to two or more factories and want quotes you can actually compare.
  • Your last sample came back wrong and nobody can point to what it was measured against.
  • You are ordering 100–1,000 pieces, where a factory gives you the least hand-holding.
  • You want the option to move factories next season without redrawing everything.

Do not buy this if

  • You are reordering an approved style from a factory that already holds your approved spec.
  • You are buying ready stock or private-label blanks with only a logo change — you need a print spec, not a pack.
  • The design is not settled. Sort the silhouette first; a pack drawn around a moving target gets redrawn.
  • You want an hourly designer on call. We quote fixed, per pack, and that is the whole arrangement.
