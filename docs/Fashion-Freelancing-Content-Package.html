Part 4 — SEO Content Brief: The 10

  • These are directional, not final keywords. Validate each primary target in a keyword tool (with India as location where marked) for real volume and low difficulty before assigning. Kill anything secretly competitive.
  • B2B fashion is long-tail by nature. Volumes are small but intent is surgical — one ranked article can feed real briefs. Expect months, not weeks.
  • Prioritize by intent, not volume. A founder about to commission a tech pack is worth more than a thousand students researching fashion careers.
  • The India + factory-language angle is the edge. US content mills write for hobbyists; almost nobody writes for Indian founders talking to Indian factories in factory vocabulary. That gap is where this site wins.

Priority tiers

  • Tier 1 — do first, transactional, feeds a service page: Topics 1, 2, 5, 8
  • Tier 2 — winnable informational, India-first: Topics 3, 4, 6, 9
  • Tier 3 — support content, lower ceiling: Topics 7, 10

1. "How much does a tech pack cost in India?" — honest cost explainer Tier 1

  • Old head term: "what is a tech pack" (saturated by US blogs).
  • New target: cost and commissioning queries — "tech pack cost India", "tech pack designer price", "how much to charge for a tech pack".
  • Intent: A founder with a design, deciding whether to commission. This is the buyer.
  • Angle: Honest market ranges and what drives price (garment complexity, size range, colorways) — without publishing our own price list; the CTA is the fixed 24h quote.
  • Feeds: Tech Pack page.

2. "What does a factory need before they'll quote you?" — checklist Tier 1

  • Target: "what to send a clothing manufacturer", "how to approach a garment factory India", "minimum order quantity clothing manufacturer India".
  • Intent: Founder about to email factories — the exact moment they discover they need a tech pack.
  • Angle: Real factory-side vocabulary (spec sheet, BOM, cutter's must); include the rejection reasons factories actually cite.
  • Feeds: Tech Pack + Pattern CAD pages.

3. "How to start a clothing brand in India — the production path" Tier 2

  • Old head term: "how to start a clothing brand" (global, saturated).
  • New target: India-specific production sequence queries — "clothing brand from scratch India", "sampling to production process".
  • Angle: The design → tech pack → sample → grading → bulk journey, honestly costed in time, with each step linking its service page. Pillar piece for the internal-link cluster.
  • Feeds: whole site; the homepage journey.

4. "Gerber vs Lectra vs DXF — which pattern format does your factory use?" Tier 2

  • Target: "DXF AAMA pattern format", "convert Gerber to Lectra", "pattern file formats apparel".
  • Why winnable: Tiny competition; searched by production managers with budgets. Nobody else writes this in plain language.
  • Feeds: Pattern CAD page (its format wall answers the question the article raises).

5. "3D sampling vs physical sampling — cost and time honestly compared" Tier 1

  • Target: "3D garment sampling cost", "CLO3D sampling service", "reduce sampling costs apparel".
  • Angle: Honest comparison including when physical sampling still wins (the honesty is the differentiator and matches the page's tone). Anchor facts to published industry results with named sources only in the article context — never as implied client work.
  • Feeds: 3D Virtual Sampling page (best converter after the interactive model).

6. "Half-drop, tossed or full-drop? Repeat types your printer expects" Tier 2

  • Target: "half drop repeat pattern", "seamless repeat for fabric printing", "print file requirements textile mill".
  • Angle: Visual explainer using the same diagrams as the service page; ends on the seam-audit concept.
  • Feeds: Seamless Pattern page.

7. "Is AI product photography allowed on Amazon and Myntra?" — policy explainer Tier 3

  • Target: "AI generated product images Amazon policy", "AI model photos Myntra".
  • Caution: Platform policies change — date-stamp the article, review quarterly, and never claim blanket permission. The honest, updated answer is the moat.
  • Feeds: AI Photography page.

8. "Amazon listing optimization for clothing — India" Tier 1

  • Target: "Amazon listing optimization clothing India", "Myntra catalog requirements", "size chart returns reduction".
  • Intent: Seller with live SKUs and a returns problem — consultation-ready.
  • Angle: The fit-information → returns connection; attribute completeness as hidden search. The listing-anatomy visual from the service page reused as article graphics.
  • Feeds: E-Com Listing page (direct conversion).

9. "WhatsApp customer support for boutiques — what to automate and what not to" Tier 2

  • Target: "WhatsApp business chatbot boutique", "automate Instagram DMs store India".
  • Angle: Honest triage — the repetitive 80% vs the human 20% (mirrors the service page's framing). The anti-hype tone stands out in an AI-slop category.
  • Feeds: AI Agent page.

10. "Version9_final_FINAL.psd — the real cost of scattered brand files" Tier 3

  • Target: "brand style guide small business", "brand consistency social media".
  • Angle: Story-led, shareable; the system-first argument from the Graphic Design page expanded with a template checklist lead magnet.
  • Feeds: Graphic Design page + email capture.

Two structural moves that matter more than any single article

  1. Fix the pipe before pouring water. The contact forms currently store leads in the visitor's own browser (localStorage) — no article should be published before form submissions actually reach you. This is the single highest-ROI task on the site.
  2. Publish the trust infrastructure first or in parallel: a real About page with the actual team, the Terms and Privacy pages (Parts 2–3 of this document, lawyer-reviewed), and 2–3 real studio proof samples to replace placeholder slots. On a B2B services site this is the ceiling on everything above.

Validation checklist before you assign

  • [ ] Drop or re-cut anything that comes back competitive.
  • [ ] Make sure every Tier 1 piece links to its service page and the quote form.
  • [ ] Don't publish Topic 7 without a policy-review date on the page.
  • [ ] Don't publish anything until the contact form backend is live.
